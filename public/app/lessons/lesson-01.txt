[section]
Basic Words Every Builder Must Know

[word]
Computer

[meaning]
A machine that shows information, saves information, and runs programs.

[why]
Everything you build will live on a computer.
It is your workspace, your toolbox, and your workshop.

[example]
A laptop or desktop is a computer.
Your phone is also a computer, just smaller.

---

[word]
File

[meaning]
A single piece of information saved on your computer.

[why]
Everything you build is made of files.
Lessons, pictures, notes, code, and quizzes are all files.

[example]
A picture is a file. A note is a file.

---

[word]
Folder

[meaning]
A container that holds files and other folders.

[why]
Folders keep your work organized so nothing gets lost.

[example]
A backpack is like a folder. It holds your things in one place.

---

[word]
Project

[meaning]
A big folder that holds everything you are building.

[why]
A project keeps all your work together so you can find it easily.

[example]
A binder full of papers is like a project.

---

[word]
Organizing

[meaning]
Putting things in the right place so you can find them later.

[why]
Good organizing makes building easier.
Bad organizing makes building confusing.

[example]
Putting your tools back in the toolbox.

---

[section]
Understanding File Endings

[intro]
Every file has an ending.
The ending tells the computer what kind of file it is.
If you save something with the wrong ending, the computer will not know what to do with it.

These endings matter more than you think.

[word]
.html

[meaning]
A file that holds the structure of a webpage.

[why]
It tells the computer what to show on the screen.

[example]
A webpage is built from an .html file.

---

[word]
.css

[meaning]
A file that controls the appearance of a webpage.

[why]
It tells the computer how things should look:
colors, sizes, spacing, and style.

[example]
Changing the color of text happens in a .css file.

---

[word]
.js

[meaning]
A file that holds instructions for behavior.

[why]
It tells the computer what to do when something happens.

[example]
When you click a button and something changes, that is .js.

---

[word]
.json

[meaning]
A file that holds structured information.

[why]
It is used to store lists, settings, and organized data.

[example]
A list of lessons can be stored in a .json file.

---

[word]
.txt

[meaning]
A file that holds plain text.

[why]
It is simple, safe, and perfect for lessons, quizzes, and notes.

[example]
This lesson is a .txt file.

---

[section]
Why Organization Is the Most Important Skill You Will Ever Learn

[meaning]
Organization is not optional.
It is the difference between:
- A builder who can create anything
- A beginner who gets lost and gives up

[why]
When your files are organized:
- You always know where things are
- You never lose your work
- You can build faster
- You can fix mistakes easier
- You can understand your own project

When your files are NOT organized:
- You get confused
- You lose progress
- You break things without knowing why
- You get frustrated
- You quit

[example]
Imagine trying to cook a meal with:
- The salt in the bathroom
- The pan in the garage
- The spoon under your bed

You would never finish.

A project works the same way.

[steps]
1. Choose a place on your computer where you will keep your work.
2. Create a new folder.
3. Name it: the-code
4. This folder is your project.
5. Everything you build will live inside it.
6. Inside your project, create folders for:
   - pages
   - quizzes
   - glossary
   - help
   - images
7. Keep everything in the right place.
8. Never mix things.
9. Never leave files loose.
10. Treat your project like a toolbox.

[practice]
Say these out loud:
- “A file is one thing.”
- “A folder holds files.”
- “A project is a big folder.”
- “The ending tells the computer what the file is.”
- “Organizing keeps me from getting lost.”

Now answer these:
1. What is a file?
2. What is a folder?
3. What is a project?
4. Why does organizing matter?
5. Why does the ending of a file matter?
