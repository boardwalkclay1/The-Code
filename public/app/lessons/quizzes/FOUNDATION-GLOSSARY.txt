[title]
Foundation Glossary — The Words You Must Know Before You Build Anything

[intro]
Before you can build an app, a website, or any kind of digital project,
you must understand the basic words that describe the things you will be working with.
These words are simple, but they are powerful.
They are the foundation of everything you will learn later.

Each word below is explained slowly, clearly, and with real examples.
Take your time. Read each one. Let it sink in.

[word]
Computer

[meaning]
A machine that shows information, saves information, and runs programs.

[why]
Everything you build will live on a computer.
It is your workspace, your toolbox, and your workshop.

[example]
A laptop or desktop is a computer.
Your phone is also a computer, just smaller.

---

[word]
File

[meaning]
A single piece of information saved on your computer.

[why]
Everything you build is made of files.
Lessons, pictures, notes, code, and quizzes are all files.

[example]
A picture is a file. A text document is a file.

---

[word]
Folder

[meaning]
A container that holds files and other folders.

[why]
Folders keep your work organized so nothing gets lost.

[example]
A backpack is like a folder. It holds your things in one place.

---

[word]
Project

[meaning]
A big folder that holds everything you are building.

[why]
A project keeps all your work together so you can find it easily.

[example]
A binder full of papers is like a project.

---

[word]
Organizing

[meaning]
Putting things in the right place so you can find them later.

[why]
Good organizing makes building easier.
Bad organizing makes building confusing.

[example]
Putting your tools back in the toolbox.

---

[word]
File Ending

[meaning]
The letters at the end of a file name that tell the computer what type it is.

[why]
The computer reads files differently based on their ending.

[example]
index.html, style.css, script.js

---

[word]
.html

[meaning]
A file that holds the structure of a webpage.

[why]
It tells the computer what to show on the screen.

[example]
A webpage is built from an .html file.

---

[word]
.css

[meaning]
A file that controls the appearance of a webpage.

[why]
It tells the computer how things should look:
colors, sizes, spacing, and style.

[example]
Changing the color of text happens in a .css file.

---

[word]
.js

[meaning]
A file that holds instructions for behavior.

[why]
It tells the computer what to do when something happens.

[example]
When you click a button and something changes, that is .js.

---

[word]
.json

[meaning]
A file that holds structured information.

[why]
It is used to store lists, settings, and organized data.

[example]
A list of lessons can be stored in a .json file.

---

[word]
.txt

[meaning]
A file that holds plain text.

[why]
It is simple, safe, and perfect for lessons, quizzes, and notes.

[example]
This glossary is a .txt file.

---

[word]
Path

[meaning]
The address of a file or folder inside your project.

[why]
It tells the computer where something lives.

[example]
public/app/pages/level-easy/lesson-01.txt

---

[word]
Command

[meaning]
A short instruction you type into a terminal.

[why]
Commands let you control your computer with words instead of clicking.

[example]
cd means “go into a folder.”

---

[word]
Terminal

[meaning]
A window where you type commands.

[why]
It gives you more control and speed than clicking.

[example]
Git Bash is a terminal.

---

[word]
GitHub

[meaning]
A website where you store your projects online.

[why]
It keeps your work safe and lets you share it with others.

[example]
Your project can live on GitHub so others can see it.

---

[word]
Clone

[meaning]
To make a copy of a project from GitHub onto your computer.

[why]
It lets you work on projects that live online.

[example]
You can clone your own project or someone else’s.

---

[word]
Save

[meaning]
To store your work so it does not disappear.

[why]
Saving keeps your progress safe.

[example]
When you close your computer, saved work stays. Unsaved work disappears.

---

[word]
Progress

[meaning]
The steps you have completed so far.

[why]
Tracking progress helps you see how far you’ve come.

[example]
If you finish Lesson 1, your progress shows it.

---

[word]
IndexedDB

[meaning]
A place inside your browser where your progress can be saved.

[why]
It remembers what you have done, even if you close the browser.

[example]
Your completed lessons and quiz scores live in IndexedDB.

---

[closing]
You now understand the basic words that every builder must know.
You are ready to begin the real lessons.
Take your time. You are not alone.
There is a whole world of builders out here.
And now, you are one of them.
