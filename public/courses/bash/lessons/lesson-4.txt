LESSON 4 — WHERE YOU ARE (pwd)
(20‑part ultra‑beginner lesson)

1. The command **pwd** stands for “print working directory.” It tells you exactly where you are inside the computer’s folder system. This is your current location.

2. Computers organize files in a tree structure. At the top is the root, and beneath it are folders inside folders. pwd shows you the exact branch you are standing on.

3. When you open a terminal, you are placed somewhere automatically. You are not told where. pwd is how you find out.

4. pwd prints a path. A path is a map of folders separated by slashes. Each slash represents a step deeper into the system.

5. A typical path looks like:  
   `/Users/yourname/Documents`  
   This means you are inside the Documents folder.

6. pwd does not move you anywhere. It only reports your location. It is a pure information command.

7. Knowing where you are is essential. Every command you run depends on your current location. This is why pwd is one of the first commands you learn.

8. pwd helps you avoid mistakes. If you think you are in one folder but are actually in another, you might delete or modify the wrong files. pwd prevents this.

9. pwd is used constantly by beginners. It builds confidence and orientation. It helps you understand the system’s structure.

10. pwd is also used by professionals. Even experts check their location before running important commands. It is a habit that never goes away.

11. pwd teaches you to think spatially. You begin to understand the computer as a physical space. This mental model makes navigation easier.

12. pwd is instant. It responds immediately with your location. This speed makes it a reliable tool.

13. pwd works the same on every system. Linux, macOS, servers, cloud machines — all use pwd. This makes it a universal skill.

14. pwd is safe. It cannot break anything. It only prints information.

15. pwd is the foundation of navigation. Before you move, you must know where you are. pwd gives you that starting point.

16. pwd helps you understand relative paths. A relative path is a location based on where you currently stand. pwd makes these paths make sense.

17. pwd helps you understand absolute paths. An absolute path starts from the root of the system. pwd shows you the full absolute path to your current location.

18. pwd is a command you will use thousands of times. It becomes second nature. It is one of the simplest and most important tools in bash.

19. pwd builds confidence. When you know where you are, you feel in control. This is the first step toward mastery.

20. Now that you know how to find your location, you are ready to learn how to see what is around you. That is where the **ls** command comes in.
