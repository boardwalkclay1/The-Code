# GITHUB LESSON 22 — WHAT A GITHUB REPOSITORY IS

=== PART 1 ===
A repository is a project folder stored on GitHub.

=== PART 2 ===
It contains your files.

=== PART 3 ===
It contains your folders.

=== PART 4 ===
It contains your project history.

=== PART 5 ===
It contains your saved moments (commits).

=== PART 6 ===
It contains your branches (different versions of your project).

=== PART 7 ===
It contains your settings.

=== PART 8 ===
It contains your project description.

=== PART 9 ===
It contains your contributors list.

=== PART 10 ===
It contains your project’s timeline.

=== PART 11 ===
A repository can be public or private.

=== PART 12 ===
Public means anyone can see it.

=== PART 13 ===
Private means only you (and people you choose) can see it.

=== PART 14 ===
A repository always starts empty.

=== PART 15 ===
You add files to it manually or through Git.

=== PART 16 ===
You can delete files at any time.

=== PART 17 ===
You can rename files at any time.

=== PART 18 ===
You can organize files into folders.

=== PART 19 ===
A repository is the core of your GitHub experience.
