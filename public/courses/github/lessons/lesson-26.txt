# GITHUB LESSON 26 — REPOSITORY SETTINGS

=== PART 1 — [Repository Settings](ca://s?q=Repository_settings_explained) ===
Repository settings control how your project behaves.  
They let you configure visibility, rules, and options.  
This is where deeper customization happens.

=== PART 2 — [Access Control](ca://s?q=Access_control_GitHub) ===
You can choose who can see your project.  
Public means everyone can view it.  
Private means only approved people can access it.

=== PART 3 — [Branch Settings](ca://s?q=Branch_settings_GitHub) ===
You can protect branches from unwanted changes.  
This prevents accidental edits.  
It keeps your main branch stable.

=== PART 4 — [Description and Topics](ca://s?q=Repository_description_topics) ===
You can add a description to explain your project.  
You can add topics to categorize it.  
This helps others find and understand your work.

=== PART 5 — [Default Branch](ca://s?q=Change_default_branch) ===
You can change which branch is the default.  
This affects what people see first.  
It also affects where new work begins.

=== PART 6 — [Delete Repository](ca://s?q=Delete_repository) ===
You can delete your repository from settings.  
GitHub asks for confirmation to prevent mistakes.  
This action cannot be undone.

=== PART 7 — [Transfer Ownership](ca://s?q=Transfer_repository) ===
You can transfer your project to another user or organization.  
This moves full control to someone else.  
It is useful for team transitions.

=== PART 8 — [Collaborators](ca://s?q=Add_collaborators) ===
You can add people who can edit your project.  
Collaborators can contribute directly.  
This is essential for teamwork.

=== PART 9 — [Webhooks](ca://s?q=Webhooks_explained) ===
Webhooks send updates to other tools when your project changes.  
They automate tasks.  
They are optional for beginners.

=== PART 10 — [Secrets](ca://s?q=GitHub_secrets) ===
Secrets store sensitive information securely.  
They are used for automation.  
They are hidden from public view.

=== PART 11 — [Pages](ca://s?q=GitHub_Pages_explained) ===
GitHub Pages lets you turn your project into a website.  
You enable it in settings.  
It publishes your content online.

=== PART 12 — [Actions Settings](ca://s?q=Actions_settings) ===
You can enable or disable GitHub Actions.  
Actions automate workflows.  
They run tasks for you.

=== PART 13 — [Security Settings](ca://s?q=Security_settings_GitHub) ===
Security settings help protect your project.  
They scan for vulnerabilities.  
They alert you to risks.

=== PART 14 — [Insights Settings](ca://s?q=Insights_settings) ===
Insights show project activity.  
They help you understand usage and contributions.  
They are useful for larger projects.

=== PART 15 — [Repository Template](ca://s?q=Repository_template) ===
You can turn your repository into a template.  
Others can copy it easily.  
This is useful for reusable setups.

=== PART 16 — [Rename Repository](ca://s?q=Rename_repository) ===
You can rename your project at any time.  
GitHub updates all links automatically.  
Your content stays intact.

=== PART 17 — [Archive Repository](ca://s?q=Archive_repository) ===
Archiving makes your project read‑only.  
It prevents further changes.  
It is useful for finished work.

=== PART 18 — [Danger Zone](ca://s?q=Danger_zone_GitHub) ===
The Danger Zone contains irreversible actions.  
These include delete, transfer, and archive.  
Use them carefully.

=== PART 19 — [Why Settings Matter](ca://s?q=Why_settings_matter) ===
Settings give you full control over your project.  
They define how others interact with it.  
They shape your project’s behavior.

=== PART 20 — [Next Lesson](ca://s?q=Start_Lesson_27) ===
Next lesson explains GitHub Issues.  
Issues help you track tasks and problems.  
They are essential for project management.
